Free bid leveling sheet template

Bid leveling means comparing subcontractor bids on the same scope, apples to apples. You line up each bidder's base bid and alternates, price anything one bidder left out that the others included, compare the adjusted totals, and check exclusions, schedule, insurance, license and references before you choose. This template handles up to five bidders, as an Excel file that adds it up and ranks them for you, or a printable PDF.

What's on the bid leveling sheet

  • Header: project, trade or scope package, bid date, and who prepared it.
  • Price rows for each bidder: base bid, alternate 1, alternate 2, scope gaps priced in, and the adjusted total.
  • Check rows: addenda acknowledged, plans and revision bid, exclusions to watch, schedule and start date, insurance (COI) on file, license verified, and references or past work.
  • Decision: the rank, your recommended bidder and why, and the questions to send bidders before award.

How to level bids, step by step

  1. Confirm everyone bid the same plans, revision and addenda.
  2. Read every exclusion. Note anything one bidder excluded that the others included.
  3. Price each gap and add it to that bidder's number.
  4. Add only the alternates you plan to accept.
  5. Compare adjusted totals, then check schedule, insurance, license and references.
  6. Send your questions before you award, not after.

The formula

Adjusted total = base bid + accepted alternates + the cost of anything a bidder left out that others included.

Questions builders and trades ask

What is bid leveling in construction?

Comparing subcontractor bids on an equal basis: same documents, same scope, with any gaps priced in, so the totals can be compared fairly before you choose.

Why isn't the lowest bid always the best?

A low number often means something's missing: an exclusion, an older plan revision, or a schedule that doesn't work. Leveling shows what each bid really costs once the gaps are priced in.

How many bids should I get?

Enough to compare: three when you can. One bid tells you a price; three tell you the market.

What is a scope gap?

Work one bidder left out that the others included or that the job needs. Price it and add it to that bidder's total so every bid covers the same work.

Does the Excel version rank the bids?

Yes. Enter each bidder's base bid, alternates and gaps, and it adds up the adjusted totals and ranks them from lowest to highest. Price is only part of the decision, so check the other rows before you award. It opens in Google Sheets too.
