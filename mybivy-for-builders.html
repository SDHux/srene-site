The problem

Small builders run on texts, spreadsheets and memory.

Construction platforms built for large GCs assume a project coordinator and a big software budget. Most private builders have a truck, a phone, and a client who wants an update.

The daily report lives in somebody's truck

Who was on site, what got done and what held things up ends up in a notebook, a text thread or nowhere at all.

Your homeowner calls because they can't see progress

Every "how's it going?" call is time off the job, and a sign the client feels out of the loop.

Plans and docs are always the wrong revision

Drawings, specs and submittals scattered across email attachments and phones on site.

Crews and subs aren't on the same page

Tomorrow's schedule, safety reminders and rain-day plans get lost, or never reach the crew in their own language.

Five things, done well

Everything a job needs, nothing it doesn't.

01

Daily reports in minutes

Weather, subs on site, crew counts, work completed and photos, filed from the job site before you leave.

02

Projects and milestones

Every job on one screen, with milestones from site work to punch list and a clear view of what's next.

03

Documents, plans and photo markup

The current revision, always. Mark up drawings and site photos and share them with the people who need them.

04

Crew announcements in their language

Schedules, safety reminders and rain-day plans sent to the whole crew, translated for the people reading them.

05

A homeowner portal

Your client sees progress, photos and next steps on their own time, without calling you to ask.

The homeowner portal
