From Rep to Sales Leader

Three steps up the ladder are the same skills rebalancing. Then the job changes twice: once at Enterprise, and much harder at the manager seat. Here is what actually moves, and where.

Each column sums to 100%. Widths show what the role is measured on, not what it is allowed to touch. The two shaded zones are the transitions where the mix genuinely breaks rather than drifts.

Activity & mechanicsVolume, call control, objection handling, CRM hygiene. Never disappears, stops being the scoreboard.
Deal craftDiscovery, qualification, business case, negotiation, close planning. Harder at every level, not bigger.
Pipeline managementCarrying thirty open deals, coverage math, forecasting a book of volume. Peaks at Mid-Market.
Account strategyDeep org mapping on a handful of named accounts, land and expand, tracking who moved where.
Internal orchestrationDirecting the SE, legal, security, exec sponsors, partners. People who do not report to you.
People leadershipCoaching, hiring, performance management, retention. Zero before the manager seat.
Systems & operating cadenceForecast discipline, inspection rhythm, running a repeatable weekly mechanism.

What the shape is telling you

Enterprise is not a bigger Mid-Market job

Pipeline management halves and account strategy triples. The Mid-Market rep wins by working thirty deals well. The Enterprise rep wins by knowing six organizations better than the people inside them. Those are different skills that happen to share a job title family.

Deal craft holds its share and gets much harder

It sits near forty percent from SMB through Enterprise. That flatness is misleading: the enterprise version involves a buying committee, a procurement gate, and a cycle that outlives your memory of the first call. Same share of the job, different degree of difficulty.

Internal orchestration is the only rehearsal you get

Running the SE, pulling in legal and security, managing an exec sponsor. It is the one skill on the entire IC ladder that means directing people who do not report to you, and it is the only band that carries across the cliff almost intact. If you want to be a manager, this is the part of the Enterprise job to volunteer for.

Forty-five percent of the manager job did not exist before

People leadership and operating cadence are zero in every role beneath it. There is no seat on the IC ladder where you practice either one, and nobody has ever assessed you on them.

The promotion is a subtraction

Deal craft drops from forty percent to eighteen. You do not add management to what you already do. You give up the work you are best at to make room for work you have never done. That trade is the job.

Two honest caveats. Titles vary by company, and a Mid-Market AE at a 5,000 person org may carry more than an Enterprise AE at a startup. The percentages are directional, drawn from how these roles are scoped and compensated, not from a survey. Both are worth stating on the page rather than implying precision that is not there.
