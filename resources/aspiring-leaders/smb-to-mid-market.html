Toolbox · Transition 2 of 4

SMB AE to Mid-Market AE

One buyer becomes three. The cycle outlives your memory of the first call. Volume stops being a virtue and becomes a management problem.

The first transition where a new band appears. Read time about six minutes.

01 / What moves

The mix, before and after

Skill bandBeforeAfter
Activity & mechanics38%22%
Deal craft42%44%
Pipeline management20%26%
Account strategy0%8%

Deal craft barely moves, which is misleading. It holds the same share while getting materially harder, because the thing you are now doing well involves more than one person and more than one month.

The real change

Pipeline management becomes the largest it will ever be. Twenty-five to thirty-five live deals is more than anyone can hold in their head, and the reps who fail here do not fail on skill. They fail because they kept running an eight-deal process on a thirty-deal book.

02 / What to stop

The part nobody says out loud

Two of these are habits. Two are beliefs.

  • Stop relying on memory. At SMB you could hold every deal in your head and it worked. At thirty deals it silently stops working, and you will not notice until three of them go quiet at once.
  • Stop trying to close on the first call. The reflex that made you efficient now makes you look like you are not listening. There is a second and a third person in this decision and neither has met you.
  • Stop treating every deal as equally worth your time. This is the belief that costs the most. Thirty deals do not deserve thirty equal hours. Deciding where not to spend Tuesday is now a real skill.
  • Stop forecasting on feel. You got away with it on volume. You will not get away with it when eight deals make your quarter and one of them is a fiction you like.

03 / What to practice

From the seat you are in now

The mid-market skills are all learnable from an SMB seat with deliberate effort.

  • Forecast yourself and check. Every Monday, write down which deals will close this quarter. Check it at the end. Almost nobody does this and it is the single fastest way to find out what you actually believe.
  • Write down the decision process. For your five largest open deals, write who signs, in what order. You will find you do not know, which is the point.
  • Triage on purpose. Split your pipeline into three tiers and assign yourself an hours budget per tier. Hold it for a month.
  • Find the second person. On every deal over a certain size, get one real conversation with someone who is not your main contact. This is multi-threading at its simplest and it is the habit that carries you to Enterprise.

04 / What proves it

Artifacts you can build before the title

One artifact here beats anything else you could build.

The personal forecast log

Two quarters, brutal

Your call on every deal, week by week, against what actually happened. Not the CRM's number, yours.

Almost no rep has this and every sales leader wants to see it. It answers the only question that matters about a mid-market rep, which is whether you know what you know.

The decision map

Five deals, one page each

Who signs, in what order, who can stop it, and how you found out. Mark clearly which parts you verified and which you assumed.

The assumed/verified split is what makes it credible. A clean map with no assumptions marked reads as fiction to anyone who has run deals.

05 / What you will be asked

The interview, and what each question is testing

  • How accurate is your forecast?Whether you have ever measured it. The answer 'pretty good' is a fail.
  • How do you decide where to spend Tuesday?Whether you triage or react. Reps who work whatever is loudest describe their week chronologically.
  • Tell me about a deal where you were single threaded.Whether you know the failure mode by experience or by vocabulary.
  • What is in your pipeline that you know is not real?Honesty under mild pressure. Everyone has one. Saying so is more impressive than a clean answer.

How you will know

One test

Look at your pipeline and name the three deals you are carrying because removing them would feel bad.

If you can do that without flinching, you are managing a book rather than collecting one.
