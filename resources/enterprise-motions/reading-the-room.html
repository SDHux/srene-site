Reading the Room

Most reps show up ready to explain how the product works. The AEs who win at the executive level show up able to explain how the business works, and where the product moves the numbers the CFO is already staring at.

Technical depth answers "how it works." Financial fluency answers "how it impacts the P&L." Only one of those gets you back in the room.
01

The three documents

Before a call with a VP or above, skim these in order. Fifteen minutes total. You are not building a model. You are finding the one or two numbers worth asking about.

Income Statement

The P&L

  • Revenue growth: accelerating or decelerating YoY?
  • Gross margin %: the first place cost pressure shows up
  • Opex (SG&A, R&D) as a % of revenue: spending discipline or investment mode?
  • Operating margin: the bottom line the CFO is paid to defend
Annual Filing

The 10-K

  • MD&A section: management's own narrative on what moved and why
  • Risk Factors: read the ones that are new this year, not boilerplate
  • Segment reporting: which business line is carrying or dragging the rest
  • Cash Flow → Investing: where CapEx actually lives
Quarterly Ritual

Earnings Call

  • Prepared remarks: the official story, written by IR and legal
  • Q&A: where analysts push and the real anxiety leaks out
  • Listen for "headwinds," "discipline," "reinvestment": each signals a different mandate
  • Guidance change: raised, lowered, or reiterated tells you their confidence
02

Two signals worth knowing cold

You don't need to read like an analyst. You need to recognize these two patterns on sight, because each one points straight at a mandate someone in the building already owns.

Margin Squeeze

Cost pressure
What it looks like Gross or operating margin falling quarter over quarter while revenue holds flat or grows. Costs are outrunning sales.
Where to spot it Compare gross margin % across the last 3-4 quarters in the P&L. A steady downward slope is the tell, not one bad quarter.
What it signals Pricing power is eroding, input or labor costs are rising, or the business is discounting to hold volume. Someone owns fixing this.
The question it earns you "I noticed gross margin has come down three quarters running. Is that mix, cost, or pricing?"

CapEx Spike

Investment cycle
What it looks like A sharp YoY jump in capital expenditures or capitalized software, visible in the investing section of the cash flow statement.
Where to spot it Cash Flow from Investing Activities in the 10-K, or CFO commentary on "elevated capital investment" on the earnings call.
What it signals Budget has already been approved for a modernization or infrastructure cycle. The money exists, the question is what it buys.
The question it earns you "You've stepped up capital investment this year. Where does systems and infrastructure sit in that plan?"

04

Before the executive call

01Pull the last 3 quarters of gross and operating margin. Flag the direction, not just the number.
02Skim the MD&A for the sentence where management explains the quarter in their own words.
03Check the Investing section of Cash Flow for a CapEx jump. That's committed budget, not a wish.
04Read the risk factors that are new this year. They tell you what's keeping the board up at night.
05Listen to (or skim the transcript of) the Q&A section, not just prepared remarks. That's where the real concern shows.
06Pick one signal and one question. Don't walk in with five. Walk in with the one that lands.
