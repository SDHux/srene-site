The Numbers They Say

The terms you will actually hear on an earnings call, in a 10-K, or from a CFO across the table, in plain language. Every entry answers two questions: why does this matter to a sale, and what do you actually say when you hear it.

Why it matters to you What to say in the room
01

The Financial Statements

The core P&L vocabulary. These are the lines a CFO watches every single quarter.

RevenueTop line

Total sales before any costs are subtracted. The starting number everything else is measured against.

WhyTells you if the business is growing, and how fast, which sets the tone for how much appetite there is to spend.
Use it"Your revenue growth has held steady even as the market's slowed. What's driving that resilience?"
Gross Margin% of revenue

Revenue minus the direct cost of delivering the product or service, shown as a percentage. What's left before overhead and sales costs.

WhyThe first place cost pressure or pricing weakness shows up. A slipping gross margin is an early warning sign, not a late one.
Use it"I noticed gross margin has come down a few quarters running. Is that mix, input cost, or pricing pressure?"
Operating MarginEBIT

What's left of revenue after gross costs and all operating expenses, like sales, marketing, and R&D. The profit from running the core business, before interest and taxes.

WhyThis is the number the CFO is personally judged on. A shrinking operating margin means someone above your buyer is asking hard questions.
Use it"How does this initiative need to show up in operating margin for it to get funded?"
SG&AOpex line

Selling, General & Administrative expenses. Sales teams, marketing, HR, legal, and other overhead that isn't tied directly to making the product.

WhyYour deal is a line item inside this bucket. When SG&A is under a cost-cutting mandate, every new tool has to prove it reduces headcount need, not just adds capability.
Use it"With SG&A under pressure, this needs to pay for itself in headcount avoided, not just productivity gained."
CapExCapital spend

Capital Expenditures. Money spent on long-term assets like equipment, facilities, or capitalized software, rather than day-to-day operating costs.

WhyA CapEx jump means budget has already been approved for a modernization or infrastructure cycle. The money exists before you even showed up.
Use it"You've stepped up capital investment this year. Where does systems and infrastructure sit inside that plan?"
EBITDACash-like profit

Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. A rough proxy for the cash the core business generates, stripped of accounting and financing noise.

WhyPrivate equity owned and highly leveraged companies live and die by this number. If your buyer's owner is a PE firm, this is likely the number that matters most to them.
Use it"How does a purchase like this need to be framed for your board, on the P&L or against EBITDA?"
Free Cash FlowFCF

Cash generated by the business after operating costs and CapEx. The money actually left over to reinvest, pay down debt, or return to shareholders.

WhyCompanies tightening free cash flow are usually slowing new spend across the board, even if revenue still looks healthy on paper.
Use it"Given where cash flow priorities sit right now, is this the kind of investment that competes for capital, or sits outside that conversation?"
02

The Filings

Where these numbers actually live, and where management explains them in their own words.

10-KAnnual filing

The full annual report a public company files with the SEC. The most complete picture available of the business, audited and legally reviewed.

WhyFree, public, and released every year. Fifteen minutes with the 10-K before a big call tells you more than most discovery questions will.
Use it"I saw in your 10-K that segment X grew faster than the rest of the business. Is that where the priority is right now?"
10-QQuarterly filing

The shorter, unaudited version of the 10-K, filed three times a year between annual reports.

WhyThe freshest official picture you can get between annual reports. Useful right before a deal review with a public company customer.
Use it"Your last quarterly filing called out cost discipline. Does that reach initiatives like this one?"
MD&AManagement's own words

Management's Discussion and Analysis. The section of the 10-K or 10-Q where leadership explains, in plain language, why the numbers moved the way they did.

WhyThis is the closest thing to hearing the CFO talk before you're actually in the room. It's the language they're likely to reuse out loud.
Use it"Your MD&A mentioned reinvestment in the platform. How does that connect to what we're discussing?"
Risk FactorsWhat keeps them up

A section of the 10-K listing the things that could hurt the business. Mostly boilerplate and legal caution, but new entries each year are meaningful.

WhyA newly added risk factor is one of the only places a company admits a genuine, current concern in writing.
Use it"You added a new risk factor around talent retention this year. Is that shaping how you're thinking about this?"
Segment ReportingBusiness by division

Revenue and profit broken out by business line or division, instead of one blended company-wide number.

WhyShows you which part of the business is carrying the rest, and which part is under the most pressure to improve, which is usually where budget gets prioritized.
Use it"Your enterprise segment is outgrowing the rest of the business. Is that where you'd want us focused first?"
03

Earnings Call Language

The words management chooses out loud, once a quarter, in front of analysts who are paid to catch every hedge.

GuidanceTheir own forecast

A company's own public forecast for future revenue or earnings. Raised, lowered, or reiterated each quarter.

WhyLowered guidance means belts tighten across the org almost immediately. Raised guidance often means new budget frees up.
Use it"With guidance raised last quarter, is there room to move faster on this than we originally planned?"
Beat / MissVs. expectations

Whether actual results came in above ("beat") or below ("miss") what Wall Street analysts expected, not against the company's own internal goals.

WhyA miss, even a small one, can trigger a cost review the same week. Timing matters. Read the room before pushing a big ask right after a miss.
Use itRarely said directly. Mostly informs when you push, not what you say.
ConsensusStreet expectations

The average forecast across all the analysts who cover the stock. The bar a company is measured against each quarter.

WhyTells you what the market expects before the company even reports, which helps you predict whether the next call is likely to be tense or relaxed.
Use itBackground knowledge more than a talk track. Useful for timing sensitive conversations.
Headwind / TailwindWorking against / for

A factor hurting results (headwind) or helping them (tailwind), usually something outside management's direct control, like currency, weather, or a slow industry.

WhyTells you whether a soft number reflects a real problem or an external factor. Don't mistake a headwind for a red flag in the deal.
Use it"You called out FX as a headwind last quarter. On a constant currency basis, how does the underlying business actually look?"
FX / Constant CurrencyCurrency effect

FX means foreign exchange, the effect of currency swings on reported results. "Constant currency" strips that effect out, showing growth as if exchange rates hadn't moved.

WhyA company can look like it's slowing down when really the dollar just got stronger. Don't read an FX headwind as a business problem.
Use it"Stripping out FX, growth actually looks strong. Is international expansion still a priority given the currency noise?"
YoY / QoQComparison basis

Year-over-year compares this period to the same period last year. Quarter-over-quarter compares this period to the one right before it.

WhyAlways check which one you're looking at. A business can look great YoY and be quietly decelerating QoQ, or the reverse.
Use itMostly a self-check before you speak. Make sure the comparison you're citing is the one that actually supports your point.
