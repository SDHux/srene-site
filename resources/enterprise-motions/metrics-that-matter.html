Metrics That Matter

The numbers a buyer actually watches shift by industry. A grocery buyer worries about shrink. An apparel buyer worries about markdowns. Know which one you're across the table from, and speak to the metric they already report on, not a generic one.

Why it matters to you What to say in the room
FMCG CPG Grocery / F&B Electronics Apparel Hardgoods Softgoods
01

FMCG

Fast-Moving Consumer Goods. High volume, thin margin, and won or lost on speed and availability.

Inventory TurnsTurnover

How many times inventory is sold and replaced over a period. High turns mean efficient movement, low turns mean overstock or slow sellers.

WhyMerchandising teams are judged directly on this number. Anything that slows replenishment or accuracy gets cut fast.
Use it"If turns are the metric your merchandising team is judged on, how does faster, more accurate product data show up in that number?"
Trade SpendTrade promotion

Money paid to retailers for promotions, slotting, and shelf placement. Often 15 to 20 percent or more of gross revenue.

WhyUsually the single biggest cost line finance is trying to control. A tool that improves promotion accuracy speaks directly to it.
Use it"How much of trade spend is currently lost to deduction disputes or promotion execution errors?"
Fill RateOrder accuracy

The percentage of an order delivered on time and in full.

WhyRetailers penalize poor fill rates with chargebacks and reduced allocation. Forecasting and data accuracy drive this number directly.
Use it"Where does fill rate sit today, and what's costing you more, the chargebacks or the lost shelf space?"
On-Shelf AvailabilityOSA

The percentage of time a product is actually available for purchase when a shopper looks for it.

WhyEvery point of OSA lost walks straight to a competitor's product. Vendor scorecards increasingly tie back to it.
Use it"If OSA is part of your vendor scorecard, this is exactly the kind of gap that shows up there."
02

CPG

Consumer Packaged Goods. Brand-led, retailer-dependent, and won on data accuracy and shelf presence as much as product quality.

GDSN Sync RateData accuracy

The percentage of product data, dimensions, attributes, and images, successfully synchronized across the Global Data Synchronization Network to retailer systems.

WhyPoor sync rates cause chargebacks, delayed listings, and failed e-commerce launches. It's often an invisible cost until someone measures it.
Use it"What's your current sync rate, and how much of your chargeback total traces back to data errors?"
ChargebacksDeductions

Fees retailers deduct from payment for compliance failures, like bad data, mislabeled cases, or late shipments.

WhyThis is real cash leaving the business every week. A CFO can usually quote this number from memory.
Use it"If we cut chargebacks tied to product data errors, what's that worth annually?"
Share of ShelfCategory presence

The percentage of physical or digital shelf space a brand occupies relative to competitors in its category.

WhyCategory managers are evaluated on this directly, and it's the clearest proxy for negotiating leverage with retailers.
Use it"Where do you sit on share of shelf today, and is that trending up or down?"
Speed to ShelfTime to market

How long it takes a new product to go from creation to being live and purchasable at retail.

WhyEvery week of delay is a week of lost sales and a week a competitor can move first. Executives track launch velocity closely.
Use it"How many weeks does it take today to get a new SKU live across your top retailers?"
03

Grocery / Food & Beverage

Thin margins, perishability, and a constant balancing act between overstock and stockout.

ShrinkInventory loss

Inventory lost to spoilage, theft, damage, or error, measured as a percentage of sales.

WhyShrink eats directly into already-thin grocery margins. Anything touching freshness, forecasting, or inventory accuracy speaks to it.
Use it"How much of shrink is tied to expiration versus damage or theft?"
Days of SupplyInventory coverage

How many days current inventory will last at the current sales rate, especially critical for perishables.

WhyToo much on perishables means write-offs. Too little means stockouts. This is the balance every grocery buyer manages daily.
Use it"Where's the sweet spot for days of supply on your top-moving perishable categories?"
Basket SizeUnits per transaction

The average number of items or dollar value per customer transaction.

WhyGrowing basket size without adding new customers is one of the cheapest ways to grow revenue, which makes anything that improves discovery relevant.
Use it"Is growing basket size a priority this year, and if so, what's driving it today?"
Case Fill RateDC delivery accuracy

The percentage of ordered cases delivered complete and on time to a grocery distribution center.

WhyDistribution centers run on tight scheduling. A poor case fill rate disrupts their whole operation and invites penalties.
Use it"What's case fill rate costing you in penalties or lost allocation right now?"
04

Electronics

Fast product cycles, steep price erosion, and a channel that can hide a slowdown for a quarter or two.

Sell-Through vs. Sell-InChannel health

Sell-in is what a manufacturer ships to a retailer. Sell-through is what actually sells to the end consumer.

WhyA gap between the two means inventory is piling up somewhere in the channel, which eventually forces markdowns.
Use it"How closely are sell-in and sell-through tracking right now? A widening gap usually shows up as margin pressure a quarter or two later."
Channel InventoryDays of inventory

How much unsold product sits in the distribution channel, measured in days of supply.

WhyRising channel inventory is one of the earliest warning signs of softening demand, well before it shows up in reported revenue.
Use it"Is channel inventory trending up? That's often the first place a slowdown becomes visible internally."
Price ErosionASP decline

The rate at which average selling price falls over a product's lifecycle, common in fast-moving electronics categories.

WhyFaster, more accurate go-to-market reduces the time a product sits at full price before erosion sets in.
Use it"If speed to market compresses your price erosion window even by a few weeks, what's that worth per launch?"
Warranty / Return RatePost-sale cost

The percentage of units returned or claimed under warranty.

WhyHigh return rates often trace back to inaccurate product information, not just defects, which makes this a data problem as much as a quality one.
Use it"How much of your return rate is attributable to the product not matching what was described online?"
05

Apparel

Seasonal, fashion-driven, and margin is won or lost by how much ends up on the clearance rack.

Sell-Through RateSeason performance

The percentage of stocked inventory actually sold within a given period, usually a season.

WhyA low sell-through rate is the direct trigger for markdowns, which is where margin actually gets destroyed in apparel.
Use it"Where does sell-through sit on your last collection, and what's driving the gap to plan?"
Markdown RateMargin leak

The percentage of revenue lost to price reductions on unsold inventory.

WhyOften the single largest margin leak in an apparel business. Anything that improves forecasting or demand accuracy attacks this directly.
Use it"If we reduced markdown rate by even a few points, how does that flow through to the bottom line?"
Open-to-BuyPurchase budget

The amount of inventory budget still available to purchase in a given period, based on planned versus actual sales.

WhyBuyers live inside this number daily. A pitch that helps them plan or forecast more accurately speaks their language.
Use it"How is open-to-buy planning handled today, and where does it break down most often?"
Size Curve AccuracyAssortment fit

How closely the mix of sizes ordered matches actual demand by size.

WhyGetting the size curve wrong means stocking out of best-selling sizes while sitting on excess in others, a hidden but constant tax on revenue.
Use it"Is size curve accuracy something your planning team tracks? It's usually a bigger number than people expect."
06

Hardgoods

Furniture, tools, appliances, sporting equipment. Higher unit cost, slower turns, and a lot of capital tied up on the floor.

Days Sales of InventoryDSI

The average number of days it takes to sell through current inventory.

WhyHardgoods carry higher unit costs and slower turns than consumables, so working capital tied up in inventory is a constant finance concern.
Use it"How is DSI trending, and is that tying up more working capital than you'd like?"
Warranty Claim RatePost-sale cost

The percentage of sold units that generate a warranty claim.

WhyHigh claim rates hit both cost and reputation. Accurate specs and fit data upstream reduce the mismatched expectations that drive claims.
Use it"How much of your warranty claim volume traces back to product information being unclear at the point of sale?"
Freight Cost% of COGS

The share of cost of goods sold consumed by shipping and handling, often significant for bulky hardgoods.

WhyRising freight costs squeeze margin fast in categories with a high shipping weight-to-value ratio.
Use it"With freight running a larger share of COGS, where's the pressure to offset that coming from?"
Channel Inventory AgingWrite-down risk

How long products have sat unsold across distributor and retail channels.

WhyAging inventory in hardgoods often ends in a write-down. Early visibility prevents a bigger problem later.
Use it"Is aged channel inventory something you get visibility into today, or does it surface late?"
07

Softgoods

Home textiles, linens, and soft home goods. Seasonal like apparel, with fit and feel expectations that punish online sellers.

Seasonal ObsolescenceEnd-of-season loss

The percentage of a season's inventory that goes unsold and loses most of its value once the season ends.

WhySoftgoods are highly seasonal and fashion-influenced, so obsolescence is a direct, recurring margin hit.
Use it"How much of last season's inventory ended up written down, and what would moving faster have been worth?"
Sell-Through RateSeason performance

The percentage of stocked inventory sold within the planned window.

WhyThe metric that determines whether a season was profitable or became a clearance problem.
Use it"What's your target sell-through by this point in the season, and where are you tracking against it?"
Return RateE-commerce

The percentage of online purchases sent back, often elevated in softgoods because fit, feel, and color don't translate well online.

WhyReturns are one of the most expensive parts of e-commerce softgoods, eating margin through both logistics and restocking losses.
Use it"How much of your return volume comes down to the product not matching its online description or imagery?"
Private Label PenetrationCategory threat

The share of a retailer's softgoods category made up of their own private label versus branded product.

WhyRising private label penetration is a direct threat to branded suppliers, and it often drives urgency around differentiation and shelf presence.
Use it"Is private label share growing in this category? That usually raises the stakes on how well your brand's story comes through."
